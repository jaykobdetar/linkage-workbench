J4–JX–EE is one rigid body. On a chosen assembly branch, M1 sets the end-effector position along a curve and M2 changes the tool heading.

How could we tell which model fits?

In a side-on recording, hold M1 still and move M2. Track the centers of J4, JX and the EE pin. If J4 and JX stay in place while EE shifts beyond measurement error, the black bend changes. A stationary EE is compatible with either model if the load or friction holds a free hinge still.

Tool coordinate transforms

Coordinates of the same point in two frames

Forward takes a point from the tool frame into the world frame. Reverse takes it back. In both matrices the first two columns rotate the axes and the last column gives the translation in mm.

Analytical form

For a point with tool coordinates u and world coordinates w, let t be the end effector’s world position and R the rotation through its tool angle.

w = R u + t
u = Rᵀ (w − t)
